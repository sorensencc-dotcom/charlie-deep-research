## Findings by item

**V-1.3 — Danish emigration ledger.** No literal ledger. p26: father emigrated alone "in 1883 on his own," family followed "one and one-half years after," Sorensen "four years of age at that time" (b. Sept 7, 1881) — implies family departure ~1884-85. Genealogy detail also pp23-25.

**V-2.1 — Ship manifest / departure year.** Same passage, p26: "getting aboard ship," fellow immigrant families, arrival "landed as immigrants... at Castle Garden," then train to Erie, PA. No manifest, but a specific narrative window (~1885, age 4) that could sharpen the current "circa" framing.

**V-2.4 — Ford hire date ("circa 1905" in treatment).** **Likely correction.** p44: "I remained there [Bryant and Berry] until the middle of 1904, when I went to work for Ford Motor Company." Sorensen states mid-1904 in his own words, not 1905.

**V-3.2 — 1908 Piquette "chassis-drag."** Strong match. pp123-125: origin of the moving line — "the idea occurred to me that we could do this very much easier if we moved the chassis along... by a tow rope on the front of it." Dated: "The month of July, 1908, we spent every Sunday planning how to do this."

**V-3.5 — 1913-14 press "first theft."** Not found. Only "theft"/"stole" hits are Soviet Amtorg copying Ford tractor designs (pp620-621, 1930s) and an unrelated "stole the show" (p730, 1939). May need external press archive.

**V-4.2 — $50M V-8 retool figure.** Verbatim match. p377: "I showed him that to revamp the plant we would spend $50,000,000 in the next two years." Context: V-8 retooling ~1932, Ford's reply "Charlie, we have too much money. Let's you and I get it working."

**V-4.3 — Foundry scrap rate / "3,000-day" figure.** Not found. Extensive foundry narrative (pp61-100), no scrap-rate percentage or "3,000-day" figure.

**V-4.4 — Service Department scale.** Not substantively found. Only hit is the 1919-20 tractor/sales Service Department merger (p682), not Bennett's internal department.

**V-4.5a — Ford-of-Europe oversight dates.** Good material. Ch.8 "European Branches" (pp250-300): Perry appointed Ford agent in England 1905; Sorensen director of all European companies, "made twenty trips" annually; Dagenham formed 1928-29; personal oversight visits 1913 through mid-1930s (Cologne, Nazi Germany ~1933-34).

**V-4.5b — Soviet/German exports.** Rich chapter-length material. Ch.15 "Amtorg" (pp593-621): Soviet commission "purchased 400 Fordson tractors on March 20, 1919," continued through 1927 via Amtorg Corp NY; 1928 commission negotiated Russian manufacturing rights; Sorensen's 1929 Moscow trip. p413: Oct 10 1942 correspondence re USSR tire-factory supply. Germany: Krupp/Cologne/Ford-A.G. Berlin (pp258-282), including declining to meet Hitler via Schacht, offering resignation over Nazi pressure on the Cologne plant (~1933-34).

**V-5.2a — Willow Run "largest" superlative.** Not found. No "largest in the world"/"largest building" phrasing anywhere.

**V-5.2b — B-24 parts count.** Not found. No specific parts-count figure; transcript repeatedly cites the throughput figure "one plane per hour" (pp211, 799, 848, 1016) instead.

**V-5.4a — "Willit Run?" coinage.** Zero hits for "Willit Run" or variants.

**V-5.5 — 63-minute figure (possible misdating).** No "63 minutes" anywhere. Sorensen's own repeated figure through his March 1944 departure is "one plane per hour" (60 min), never 63. The historically-cited "one B-24 every 63 minutes" peak rate is generally dated mid/late-1944 — **after** his resignation. Consistent with the remediation plan's existing TEMPORAL_INTEGRITY flag; the transcript, written from inside the period, never reaches that figure — a negative result that supports the existing flag rather than resolving it.

**V-6.1 — Sorensen vs. Edsel culpability.** No single credit/blame passage. Scattered framing, e.g. p364: on the V-8, "Edsel was now the stylist. Fewer ideas came from the organization." Sorensen consistently positions himself as production/engineering driver, Edsel as styling/finance-facing. Worth a closer read of pp360-395 if this item becomes priority.

**V-6.2a — Ford health-decline dating.** Strong, precise dating, three data points: p17 — "By 1933 he became apprehensive... first break in his health occurred about this time... had a stroke that set him down hard"; p316/431 — "Henry Ford had a stroke in 1938. It was not generally known. Dr. McClure told Edsel and I about it"; p454 — "Henry Ford had a stroke in 1938 and another stroke followed this 1941." Explicit 1933/1938/1941 progression.

**V-6.2b — Service Dept dismissals.** Not substantively found. One tangential passage (p943) on Bennett's general hire/fire pattern with the Board, no scale/timing figures.

**V-6.3 — Federal takeover correspondence.** Not found. No hits for government seizure/commandeer language; only generic "run the plant" phrases unrelated to a takeover threat.

**V-6.4 — HFII Navy release.** Good narrative context, no hard release date/order. pp321-322 (WWI-era deferment precedent); p802 ("Henry Ford II now ensign in the Navy, stationed in Chicago," Apr 22); pp807-873 (Sorensen repeatedly lobbying for his release to Willow Run; Henry Ford himself ambivalent/hostile per pp976-980, June 1943 diary entries showing father-son friction and Sorensen personally pressing HFII on "his duty" to join). No explicit formal-release date or who arranged it beyond Sorensen's own advocacy.

**V-6.6 — Bennett "You're through" quote.** Not found. No hits near a Bennett/HFII dismissal-quote context. Consistent with this being a post-March-1944 event outside the transcript's coverage window (already handled as a V-6.5-adjacent keystone item).
